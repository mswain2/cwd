Kenzie's Apple Music Analysis

This is an analysis into how my listening trends have changed based on the year. There is also a comparison of how my total minutes listened changes based on the month.

This dataset spans from January 2022 - March 2026 and was extracted from my personal Apple Music statistics. The yearly statistics include the total listening minutes, total songs, and total artists. The monthly statistics include the total listening minutes.

Total Minutes Per Year

The first thing I wanted to look at was the total minutes per year. This gives a quick overview of how my listening has changed over the last 4 years. I chose the scatter chart because it shows the individual points really well as well as the increases and decreases.

The value for 2026 is really low compared to the rest because there were only three months of data for this year. There was a steady increase occurring that capped in 2024 before slightly decreasing for 2025. It’s hard to say exactly what influenced this. I would say that the sharp increase from 2022 to 2023 is likely from me starting college. I had more time to listen to music and was likely more stressed meaning I would listen to more music. I think it is really interesting that 2025 was a decrease from 2024 because I was finishing out junior year and starting senior year in 2025. I would have expected 2025 to be higher. It will be interesting to see where 2026 falls on this graph once the year has finished.

Total Songs Per Year

Another statistic provided by Apple Music is the total songs listened to per year. This gives an overview of how widespread my listening was for a certain year. I chose to use a lollipop chart because I think it’s a good variation of the bar chart to show the total songs per year. I also wanted to explore different visualizations for the numerous yearly statistics.

Once again, the value for 2026 is low due to a lack of data. This data is a bit more haphazard than the total listening minutes. There doesn’t appear to be a pattern of increases or decreases within the data I have. It makes sense that 2024 and 2025 have the highest values for this since they also had the highest values for total listening minutes. It appears that the more I listened to music, the more unique songs I had a chance to listen to. The value for 2022 is really shocking. I am not surprised that 2023 is lower than 2024 and 2025 since this once again reflects the values for total listening minutes. I am very surprised that, while 2023 had a drastically higher number of total listening minutes compared to 2022, 2023 actually had a lower number of total unique songs compared to 2022. It is a very small difference, but 2023 having such a lead on 2022 in listening minutes and being behind in total unique songs is really interesting. I’m not sure what would have caused this, but the deviation from the pattern is certainly intriguing.

Total Artists Per Year

Total artists per year also gives an indication to how unique my listening taste was during each year. A lot of artists indicates a variety of music. I chose to use a simple circles chart to show the total artists per year. The size of the circles reflects the number of unique albums.

2026 once again has a low/nonexistent value due to a lack of data. The data for total artists matches that of total songs. I am still quite shocked by the difference in value from total listening minutes. 2024 and 2025 are once again the highest and reflect the positions, highest and second highest respectively, that they held for total listening minutes and total songs. However, 2022 and 2023 are once again mismatched in my opinion. 2023 showed a sharp increase in listening minutes from 2022, but has lower total songs and total artists. It seems like while 2023 had a larger number of listening minutes, it had less diverse artists and albums and consisted of the same albums or songs. This would make sense due to my starting college in 2022/2023 and likely having more stress. Listening to the same songs would bring a sense of routine or comfort in a time of big changes.

Total Minutes Per Month

This is the statistic I was most interested in. The total listening minutes per month is more granular than any of the yearly statistics given by Apple Music. It can also show trends in how I listen to music based on the time of year or the environment I am in at that time. I decided to use a line chart to display this data with one line per year. I am interested to see if there are general trends across the year.

It definitely looks like there are trends across years. There are spikes in listening typically in April and November which represent exam time for each semester. It appears my music listening increases during those times likely due to having it one while working on projects or studying. There are also dips during May and December where I am usually on break and relaxing or hanging out with family and friends. There are also smaller spikes in January and September indicating an increase in listening when I return to campus. As I stated earlier, I have more time to listen to music while at school. I also noticed a bit of a spike for July. This doesn’t make as much sense to me as the other spikes and dips do. Overall, most of the years tend to follow a similar pattern throughout the months. It’s really interesting to see this visual and make connections to things happening in my life at those times and what could be influencing different increases and decreases.

Final Findings

I had a really good time exploring this data. I’ve always been interested in how my music listening statistics look since I do spend a lot of time listening to music. It was really interesting to see some how some of the songs and albums statistics differed from the pattern shown in total listening minutes for the years. A large amount of listening minutes does not necessarily indicate a diverse listening library.

I also loved seeing the patterns in my monthly listening. It is super interesting to see how changes in my life affect the way I listen to music. Sadly, Apple Music doesn’t have the statistics for total songs or albums per month, but that would also be really interesting to explore and see if there is a pattern with that data as well. I had a good time exploring different representations of data for the yearly statistics and analyzing all of the visualizations that resulted from this exploration.
